# HallPass Digital Go-to-Market Plan

## Product

**HallPass — Student Reviews & Campus Survival Guide**

## Target Early Users

The first test audience is first-year, international, and exchange students at
Rangsit University. These users face the problem most directly when choosing a
dormitory, finding affordable food, and learning unfamiliar campus procedures.
Senior students are a secondary audience because they can contribute reviews and
practical guidance.

## Main Value Proposition

HallPass helps new Rangsit University students make faster, more confident
campus decisions by collecting trusted student experiences and practical advice
in one clear place.

## Channel Plan

| Channel | Target User | Channel Message | Expected Action | Metric | Why It Fits |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| First-year/class LINE groups | First-year students | New to RSU? Compare dorms, food, and campus tips in one student-built guide. | Open landing page and try the demo | Link clicks, demo opens | Students already use class chats for immediate campus questions. |
| International College email/LINE community | International and exchange students | Settling into Rangsit University? HallPass gathers practical student reviews and survival tips in one place. | Read a guide and submit feedback | Email/link clicks, guide opens, feedback responses | This reaches students during orientation and early adjustment. |
| QR posters near dorms and cafeterias | Students making location-based decisions | Choosing where to live or eat? Scan to compare student experiences. | Scan QR and browse a category | QR visits, category clicks | The message appears at the moment the information is relevant. |
| Instagram Story/Reel | New and prospective students | Three campus questions, one shortcut: dorms, food, and survival tips with HallPass. | Visit landing page | Story link taps, landing-page views | Visual, mobile-first content suits student discovery habits. |
| Student clubs and orientation ambassadors | New students and student contributors | Help new students settle in—test HallPass and share what is missing. | Try prototype and give feedback | Demo attempts, completed feedback | Trusted peer leaders can provide feedback and encourage participation. |

## Launch Sequence

1. Publish the landing page with a working prototype CTA and privacy statement.
2. Test the full route from landing page to the HallPass prototype.
3. Create one tagged link or QR code for each outreach channel.
4. Share first with one class group and the international-student community.
5. Record views, CTA clicks, demo attempts, and feedback daily for one week.
6. Compare channel conversion and feedback quality.
7. Improve the headline, CTA, channel message, or prototype navigation based on evidence.

## Success Criteria for the First Test

- At least 50 landing-page views.
- At least 20 primary CTA clicks (40% landing-page-to-demo click rate).
- At least 15 prototype demo attempts.
- At least 10 feedback responses.
- At least 60% of feedback respondents say HallPass is useful or very useful.

These are experiment targets, not claims of current traction.

## Risks and Mitigation

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Students assume HallPass is a live production service | Label the experience as a classroom prototype and use fictional/sample data. |
| Users submit sensitive or harmful information | Display a clear data-minimization message and discourage sensitive personal content. |
| A broad message attracts users outside the intended segment | Lead with “Rangsit University” and tailor each message to a defined student group. |
| Link traffic cannot be attributed to a channel | Use a different tagged link or QR code for each channel and record the source in the tracking CSV. |
| Interest is high but prototype use is low | Test clearer CTA wording and link directly to the most relevant prototype workflow. |
| A small sample is treated as proof of market demand | Report sample size and collection method; describe findings as early evidence only. |

## Review Decision

After the first test, keep a channel if it produces at least five visits and a
CTA click rate of 25% or more. Revise or pause channels below the threshold.
Prioritize recurring feedback themes that affect the main student workflow.
