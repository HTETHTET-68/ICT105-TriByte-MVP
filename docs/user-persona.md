# Lab 04 - User Persona

## 1. Persona Name
May, Year 1 University Student

## 2. User Type
First-year student navigating dormitory, cafeteria, and campus life decisions for the first time

## 3. Background and Context
- **Where:** The problem shows up mainly around the dorm (choosing/settling into housing) and the cafeteria (finding decent, trustworthy food options), plus general "how does campus work" questions during the first weeks of term.
- **When:** Most acute in the first 1–3 months at university — 7 of 10 first-year respondents are within this window, and difficulty scores stay high (average 4.3/5) throughout that period.
- **Digital tools already used:** Friends and word-of-mouth (most common source), social media, group chats, and the university website , all informal or scattered channels rather than one trustworthy platform.

## 4. Goals
- Find trustworthy, up-to-date information about dorms and cafeterias without asking around repeatedly.
- Get through the first week of campus life without feeling lost or stressed.
- Make decisions (which dorm block, where to eat) with confidence instead of guessing.
- Spend less time (currently 30–60+ minutes per search) hunting for basic information.

## 5. Pain Points from Lab 03 Evidence

| Pain Point | Evidence ID / Respondent | Explanation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Information is scattered across multiple sources | R001, R007, R015 | Difficulty mostly rated 5/5; searching information that is vast "a few times a week" or daily and end up wasting time or feeling stressed |
| Official information is outdated | R003, R012, R018 | University website/group chat content doesn't reflect current conditions, leading to repeatedly asking other people |
| Reviews aren't written by real students | R004, R009 | Led to a bad dorm decision (R004) and a bad food choice (R009) |
| Don't know where to start looking | R014, R019 | New students (less than 1 month at university) feel stressed with no clear starting point; search "every day" |

## 6. Current Alternatives / Workarounds

| Current Alternative | Weakness / Limitation | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Asking friends | Informal, inconsistent, and not always accurate; still the top source (4 of 10 respondents) | R001, R004, R009, R015, R019 |
| Social media | Fast but unverified and scattered across many pages/groups | R007 |
| University website | Often outdated and not written from a student's day-to-day perspective | R003 |
| Group chats | Useful but disorganized, hard to search later | R012, R018 |


## 7. Design Implications
- **What HallPass should support:** A first-week guide and dorm reviews should be prioritized first as they're the two most-requested features among first-year respondents (4 and 2 mentions respectively, alongside cafeteria reviews at 3). Content must be written by real, current students to solve the "not written by real students" complaint (R004, R009).
- **What the team should avoid:** Don't rely on static, official-style content (mirrors the outdated university website problem) or on scattered, unmoderated formats (mirrors the social media/group chat problem). Avoid trying to serve all student types equally in the MVP — second- and third-year students showed low difficulty (avg. 2.0/5) and mostly "Maybe" interest, so they are not the priority.
- **Most important need for MVP:** A trustworthy, searchable "first week" starting point combined with real student-written dorm and cafeteria reviews — this directly targets the highest-severity, highest-frequency pain point (information scattered / no clear starting point) reported by first-year students.
